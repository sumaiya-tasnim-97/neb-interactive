Nudged Elastic Band — interactive toy model

Introduction

Chemical reactions, diffusion hops and conformational changes all take a system from one energy minimum to another. On the potential-energy surface, the reference route between them is the minimum-energy path (MEP): the path that runs along the valley floor, so that at every point the force perpendicular to the path is zero. The highest point on the MEP is a first-order saddle point (transition state); its height above the reactant is the potential-energy barrier ΔE‡ used in harmonic transition-state theory. The MEP is a property of the potential energy alone: at finite temperature, entropy and other free-energy effects can change which pathway is preferred and how high its barrier is. Like standard NEB, this page works on the potential-energy surface.

The nudged elastic band (NEB) method finds the MEP by relaxing a chain of intermediate configurations (“images”) connected by springs[1,2]. A plain elastic band[3] fails because springs pull the chain off the curved valley (corner-cutting) while the true force drags images down into the minima. NEB fixes both with a projection — the “nudge”: each image keeps only the perpendicular part of the true force and only the parallel part of the spring force. A stable tangent estimate[4] and the climbing-image modification[5], which drives the highest-energy image toward a first-order saddle point (the result should then be verified; see Validation), make up the method used in practice (e.g. in VASP/VTST and ASE[8,9]).

This page runs NEB live on three 2D toy surfaces, including the classic Müller–Brown potential[6], so every vector can be seen. How to use it:

  1. Press Run (or Step) and watch the white band slide into the valley. Rotate the 3D surface by dragging, or switch to the 2D contour view.
  2. Tap any image to see its unit tangent τ̂ and the net force resolved into F⊥ and F∥, with values in reduced units.
  3. Switch Method to Climbing-image NEB to converge the top image onto the saddle point, or to the plain elastic band to see why nudging is needed.
  4. Use the Convergence tab to watch the forces decay, and tighten the stopping threshold to drive F⊥ toward zero. The optimizer is quick-min[7].

How NEB works

A chain of “images” connects two energy minima[1]. Each image feels only the part of the true force perpendicular to the path, plus a spring force along the path that keeps the images evenly spaced. The band relaxes onto the minimum-energy path (MEP); the climbing-image variant pushes the highest image up to the saddle point. The 3D view draws the energy surface E(x,y) gnuplot-style, with the tangent and force arrows lying on the surface.

For each interior image i at Ri, estimate the path tangent τ̂i (pointing toward the higher-energy neighbour)[4]. Then split the forces:

F⊥ = −∇V(Ri) + (∇V·τ̂) τ̂ Fs∥ = k (|Ri+1−Ri| − |Ri−Ri−1|) τ̂ FNEB = F⊥ + Fs∥

The “nudge” is the projection: the true force can’t drag images along the path (no sliding into minima), and springs can’t pull the band off the MEP (no corner-cutting). For CI-NEB[5] the highest image drops its springs and has its parallel force inverted, F = −∇V + 2(∇V·τ̂)τ̂, so it climbs uphill along the path and downhill across it, converging to a first-order saddle point. The page then checks the result: |∇V| below the threshold and a Hessian with one negative eigenvalue (det H < 0).

The arrows show the net force F on each image resolved against the tangent: F⊥ (red) and F∥ (blue). For NEB, F⊥ is exactly the projected true force and F∥ is exactly the spring force; for the other methods the split shows what the un-nudged forces do, e.g. a plain elastic band has a large F∥ from −∇V that drags images downhill. In 3D each arrow lies on the surface: an in-plane step d rises by ∇V·d [ε].

Things to try

  • Press Step a few times and watch the green tangents and red F⊥ arrows on each image.
  • Run NEB, then switch to CI-NEB: the top image turns gold and climbs onto the ✕.
  • Plain elastic band with high k cuts the corner and overestimates the barrier; with low k the images slide into the minima.
  • −∇V only: without the band every image just falls into a minimum.
  • Two routes: start with an arc on either side and NEB finds the local MEP nearest your guess.
  • In the 2D view, drag an image off the path mid-run and watch it get pulled back.
  • Choose Surface → Faint + target MEP: the purple guide is the converged path, computed in advance with 41 images. Press Run and watch the band settle into it while the “band → target MEP” distance drops to ~0.
